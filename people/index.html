Rajni Kumari is a Staff Scientist in Uli's lab, joining as a postdoc in 2018. Anastasiya Slaughter

Research Fellow

Anastasiya Slaughter

Research Fellow

Anastasiya received her B.A in Criminal Justice from University of Michigan in 2014. At the same time, she worked as a lab technician in the lab of Daniel Lucas where she contributed to a manuscript investigating how cytokines produced by mature hematopoietic cells regulate the vessel regeneration in the bone marrow. She continued as a graduate student in the Lucas Lab while she completed her Ph.D in Immunology at University of Cincinnati in 2023. Her thesis focused on how vessels regulate the process of emergency neutrophil production and lymphoid contraction by sensing the inflammatory cytokine Interleukin-1 and coordinate progenitor responses.

In the Steidl Lab, Anastasiya is applying her expertise in cell-to-cell crosstalk and multicolor whole tissue imaging to investigating how the stromal (non-hematopoietic) cells contribute to AML development and progression. Specifically, her project looks at how overexpression of B-catenin in bone cells (osteoblasts) induces overexpression of MDM4 (a p53 and B-catenin regulator) in hematopoietic progenitors in the context of TET2 clonal hematopoiesis which progresses to AML. Her favorite part of the project is using mouse models that mimic endogenous development of myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) which progresses to AML.
